# VIS-TRANSFER-HISTORY-REPLAY-PROPOSAL-01 — Transfer history and idempotent replay

**Status: Non-binding proposal for Owner review only.** This document analyzes the two remaining choices under approved `VIS-TRANSFER-OWNER-DECISION-RECON-04`: transfer history and replay semantics. It does not approve or authorize a schema, code, audit-policy, permission, or runtime change. Transfer remains default-deny while SEC-01 and the required implementation evidence are open.

## 1. Fixed contract and current evidence

RECON-04 fixes transfer actor/scope, eligible visit states, destination-room requirements, new destination queue number with priority preserved, preserved visit status/`ngayKham`/examination timestamps, mandatory fixed reason code and optional short note, separate `queueDate` in `Asia/Ho_Chi_Minh`, linked-work preservation, UTC technical/audit metadata, and transaction/concurrency requirements. It leaves open exactly the history model and replay outcome, including reuse of one key with a different payload; neither may be inferred from earlier proposals ([decision-gap memo](phase3-visit-transfer-decision-gaps.md#L80-L92), [transfer contract review](phase3-visit-transfer-contract-review.md#L61-L70)).

Current audit evidence constrains the choices:

- V1 defines `NhatKyHeThong` with actor, action, `chiTiet`, and time, and `NhatKyHoSoBenhAn` with visit, actor, action, and reason ([V1 audit tables](../../clinic-infrastructure/src/main/resources/migrations/V1__Initial_Clinic_Schema.sql#L1079-L1112)). V6 adds role, facility, department, resource type/ID, outcome, reason code, workstation, correlation ID, and `occurredAtUtc` to both ([V6 metadata](../../clinic-infrastructure/src/main/resources/migrations/V6__Enhance_Audit_Tables_Append_Only.sql#L3-L47)).
- V6 enforces append-only state and rejects `UPDATE`/`DELETE` on both audit tables ([V6 constraints and triggers](../../clinic-infrastructure/src/main/resources/migrations/V6__Enhance_Audit_Tables_Append_Only.sql#L69-L108)). This is a relevant integrity pattern, not evidence that a transfer-history table already exists or would inherit the same guarantees.
- `SystemAuditEntry` represents actor, role, one facility/department scope, action/resource, outcome, UTC time, workstation, and correlation ([record](../../clinic-application/src/main/java/vn/clinicmanagement/application/audit/SystemAuditEntry.java#L6-L24)). The current JDBC writer inserts audit rows in the active transaction and writes `chiTiet`/`lyDo` as `NULL`; its audit write is not a structured before/after transfer record ([writer](../../clinic-infrastructure/src/main/java/vn/clinicmanagement/infrastructure/persistence/system/JdbcAuditRepositoryImpl.java#L18-L27), [binding and transaction paths](../../clinic-infrastructure/src/main/java/vn/clinicmanagement/infrastructure/persistence/system/JdbcAuditRepositoryImpl.java#L42-L69)).
- No transfer action, history persistence, or transfer-specific mutation currently exists ([contract review](phase3-visit-transfer-contract-review.md#L41-L57)). The current correlation field is evidence for joining activity, not an idempotency key or a uniqueness guarantee; the persistence proposal likewise notes that the audit API has no transfer-specific linkage yet ([prior persistence proposal, section 5](phase3-transfer-queue-history-persistence-proposal.md#L110-L119)).
- Prior linked-work review finds that although V1 can represent services, prescriptions/results, and billing records attached to a visit, the checked-in application lacks the readers/adapters needed to prove safe handoff. Transfer must remain fail-closed until that evidence exists ([linked-work review](phase3-transfer-linked-work-eligibility-review.md#L7-L16), [implementation gaps](phase3-transfer-linked-work-eligibility-review.md#L57-L65)).
- The inventory `IdempotencyRecord` is a mutable domain model with a local-clock 24-hour default expiry; it has no demonstrated transfer consumer or durable replay path ([model](../../clinic-domain/src/main/java/vn/clinicmanagement/domain/inventory/IdempotencyRecord.java#L5-L22), [prior persistence analysis](phase3-transfer-queue-history-persistence-proposal.md#L40-L45)).

## 2. Transfer-history alternatives

All three alternatives must preserve the approved append-only audit requirements and the transaction boundary. The successful visit/location/queue mutation, required audit event, and whichever history/receipt artifact Owner selects must commit or roll back together. The existing audit port has a current-transaction append path; using an independent connection for a success event would not satisfy that atomicity requirement ([`AuditWritePort`](../../clinic-application/src/main/java/vn/clinicmanagement/application/audit/AuditWritePort.java#L3-L10), [prior transaction proposal](phase3-transfer-queue-history-persistence-proposal.md#L75-L95)).

| Option | Append-only and actor/scope/correlation evidence | Atomicity | Retrieval, retention, and tradeoffs |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Dedicated immutable transfer-history record** | Can represent structured before/after location and queue facts, reason code, actor/source scope, UTC occurrence, stable transfer identity, and correlation without overloading generic audit fields. It still needs explicit immutability enforcement and an unambiguous link to the required audit event. | Insert the transfer record, queue/location mutation, and success audit in one transaction. Any required-write failure must roll back all of them. A history-only write does not replace the required security audit. | Best fit for visit-centric history and operational queries by visit, time, actor, source/destination, and outcome; a purpose-built access path can avoid parsing log text. Adds schema and a separate controlled read/retention lifecycle. Requires decisions on sensitive fields, authorization/auditing of history reads, and retention/archive policy. |
| **Audit-only** | Existing audit metadata can evidence the actor, one role/scope pair, resource, outcome, UTC time, workstation, and correlation. It does not currently represent both source and destination, queue allocation, expected/resulting visit version, or a replay receipt. `chiTiet` exists in V1 but the current writer leaves it null; treating it as a transfer payload would be a new audit design, not existing capability. `NhatKyHoSoBenhAn` has a visit FK, but using it for transfer semantics is not an approved audit mapping. | A single audit append can be atomic with the mutation only when it uses the same active transaction. Audit write failure must fail the transfer and roll it back. | Avoids a second history artifact, but forces a choice between expanding audit payload/schema and losing structured transfer detail. Querying and retention become coupled to security-log policy; text/JSON parsing, one-scope metadata, and lack of an original-result receipt weaken retrieval and replay. Existing append-only triggers are useful, but do not provide the missing transfer fields. |
| **Combined immutable history plus audit event** | History stores structured transfer facts; audit stores who acted, in which source scope, on which visit, with result, UTC time, workstation, and correlation. A stable transfer ID or explicit relation can link them. Correlation alone is not demonstrated as unique, so it must not be treated as a deduplication constraint. | Strongest evidence only if history and audit are both required writes in the same transaction as the visit/queue mutation; failure of any one rolls back all successful artifacts. This must use the current-transaction audit path, not independent logging. | Supports both operational visit history and security audit queries, but duplicates some metadata and adds consistency, access-control, and retention coordination work. The Owner must decide whether retention periods may differ and how archival preserves the link; neither policy is currently established. |

**Non-binding recommendation for Owner consideration:** Prefer the **combined** approach if a later approved implementation needs both queryable visit transfer history and security attribution: keep structured before/after and receipt facts in an immutable transfer record, and emit the minimal successful audit event with stable linkage in the same transaction. Do not put clinical narrative or unrestricted note text in general audit payloads. Treat this as a design preference only; an Owner decision is required before implementation. This aligns with, but does not elevate to policy, the earlier persistence proposal's section 5 recommendation ([prior proposal](phase3-transfer-queue-history-persistence-proposal.md#L110-L119)).

## 3. Replay alternatives and failure cases

Replay identity must be distinct from correlation: correlation joins events from an attempt, while an idempotency key identifies a caller's requested operation across retries. A persisted receipt/fingerprint is necessary for reliable recovery; neither the current audit schema nor the unused inventory model establishes that transfer capability. The exact key namespace, payload equality rules, lifetime, and authorization on replay remain Owner choices ([prior persistence proposal, section 6](phase3-transfer-queue-history-persistence-proposal.md#L120-L142)).

| Request situation | Feasible semantics | Consequence / non-binding recommendation |
|---|---|---|
| **Same key, same payload; prior operation committed** | Return the original minimal committed receipt as a replay; return a generic already-processed conflict; or execute again. | Re-execution can allocate another number and move/version the visit again, so it defeats retry safety. Returning the original receipt avoids a repeated side effect but requires a durable key-to-payload-to-receipt mapping and authorization on each attempt. A generic conflict avoids disclosure but makes recovery harder. **For consideration:** return only the original minimal receipt, marked as replay, after revalidating the caller's authority; disclose no destination queue or patient data. |
| **Same key, different payload** | Reject as key reuse; overwrite/rebind the key; or include the payload in key identity and treat it as a separate operation. | Rebinding makes retries ambiguous and can erase the original operation's meaning. Including payload in identity lets one apparent key trigger multiple side effects. **For consideration:** preserve the first committed binding and return a stable, non-sensitive key/payload conflict for any different canonical payload; no transfer, allocation, or second success audit. |
| **Duplicate request or retry after timeout; original commit outcome unknown to client** | Ask the client to use a new key; report failure and permit a new operation; or resolve/retry using the same key. | A new key or blind resubmission can duplicate a committed transfer. **For consideration:** require the same key and payload; once the original transaction resolves, return its stored receipt if committed, or execute once if it rolled back. If still unresolved, report an indeterminate/retryable outcome without claiming success or failure. Never infer that a timeout means rollback. |
| **Concurrent duplicate while original transaction is unresolved** | Reject immediately; wait for the in-flight operation; or race and rely on a later repair. | A unique durable operation identity and transaction-level serialization can make the eventual result deterministic; the precise SQL locking, wait limit, and retry policy need implementation review. **For consideration:** wait within a bounded policy, then replay the committed receipt or proceed only after the first attempt is known to have rolled back. No partial queue/history/audit writes may survive. |
| **No committed record after a failure** | Reserve the key permanently, reserve it temporarily, or make it available for retry. | A pre-commit key reservation can outlive or conflict with rollback if it is not in the same transaction; an absent record while the original is unresolved cannot prove rollback. Owner must decide whether denied/invalid requests reserve keys and when a failed key can be reused. |

For payload comparison, a later contract needs to state exactly which normalized command fields are bound to a key (for example visit/version, destination IDs, mandatory reason code, and optional note), how normalization is defined, and whether server-derived `queueDate` is persisted with the original operation. A retry spanning midnight must not silently become a different transfer payload or alter a committed receipt. These are proposed comparison dimensions, not approved field definitions.

Under any selected replay policy, a key must not act as an authorization capability. Replay must not grant destination reads, and the returned result must not expose patient or destination queue contents. The same transaction should bind the successful location/queue mutation, committed replay receipt, chosen history record, and success audit; otherwise a timeout could leave a transfer without a recoverable receipt or an audit/history artifact without a committed transfer. Do not prescribe a particular isolation level or claim that current code already implements this protocol.

**Non-binding recommendation for Owner consideration:** Bind one key to one canonical request in a clearly defined actor/operation scope. For a committed equal request, return its original minimal receipt without repeating side effects; for a different request under that key, reject without writes; after a timeout, retry only with the same key and resolve the committed receipt versus rolled-back attempt before proceeding. Require a later implementation contract to define canonicalization, key scope/lifetime, authorization, wait/unknown-outcome behavior, and whether failed attempts reserve keys. None of these replay outcomes is approved by RECON-04.

## 4. Exact Owner decisions required

Before any implementation, the Owner must explicitly decide:

1. **History authority:** dedicated immutable transfer history, audit-only, or both; whether a success audit is always required; the canonical source of truth for transfer facts; and how exactly one successful transfer is linked to its audit evidence.
2. **History evidence and access:** which before/after, reason, actor/source-scope, destination, queue, version, UTC timestamp, correlation, and operation-identity facts are retained; which roles may query/print history; and how those reads are authorized and audited without granting destination patient/queue reads.
3. **History immutability and retention:** whether history is strictly append-only; retention period, archive/legal-hold and disposal rules; and whether audit and history may have different retention periods without breaking linkage.
4. **Idempotency namespace and lifetime:** key format and uniqueness scope (operation, actor, role, source scope, session, or another explicit combination); expiry/retention; behavior after expiry; and whether failed, denied, or invalid attempts reserve a key.
5. **Same-key/equal-payload result:** return the original minimal receipt, return a duplicate/conflict response, or another precise outcome; what fresh identity, session, assignment, and permission checks are required on replay, including after revocation or scope changes.
6. **Same-key/different-payload result:** reject with a stable conflict or select another explicit behavior; define the exact canonical payload fields and normalization/versioning rules, including optional note handling and server-derived `queueDate`.
7. **Duplicate/timeout resolution:** define concurrent-duplicate behavior, maximum wait and client-visible unknown outcome, how a retry discovers commit versus rollback, and when reuse of the same key is safe. Confirm that no timeout response asserts rollback without evidence.
8. **Atomic required writes:** identify the exact success artifacts that must share one transaction and require rollback on failure, including whether the selected history record and success audit are both mandatory. This decision must preserve RECON-04's transaction and UTC/audit requirements and must not change append-only audit behavior or weaken any runtime gate.

Until these decisions are approved and the required linked-work evidence exists, this proposal does not authorize implementation or enable transfer.
